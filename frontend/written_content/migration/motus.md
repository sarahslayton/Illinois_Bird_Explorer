The Motus Wildlife Tracking System is an international collaborative research network that uses coordinated automated radio telemetry to track the fine-scale movements of small migratory animals — including birds, bats, and insects. Researchers attach miniaturized nanotags to individual animals; as those animals travel, they are detected by a global array of automated receiver stations whenever they pass within range. The resulting data reveal migration routes, stopover sites, departure timing, and survival rates at a resolution impossible to achieve with traditional banding alone. Illinois hosts multiple Motus receiver stations, contributing to studies of shorebirds, passerines, and other taxa moving through the Mississippi Flyway.
