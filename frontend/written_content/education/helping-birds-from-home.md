## Plant Native

Native plants are the foundation of healthy bird habitat. They support the insects that nearly all terrestrial bird species depend on to feed their young, provide berries and seeds timed to migration and winter needs, and offer shelter and nesting sites that non-native plants cannot replicate. Even small native plantings can meaningfully increase the insect and food resources available to birds in your neighborhood. By replacing turf grass with native plants, planting a rain garden or a pollinator patch, or a few native shrubs along a fence line, you can create meaningful habitat in your own yard.

## Lights Out!

Most species of migratory birds migrate at night. Artificial light attracts birds, interfering with their ability to navigate and drawing them toward cities where they face an increased risk of collision with buildings and glass windows. By turning off or shielding unnecessary exterior lights during peak migration nights, especially between March 1-June 15 and August 15-November 20, you can significantly reduce this hazard.

## Window Decals

Window collisions kill an estimated 600 million to 1 billion birds in the United States every year. Birds cannot perceive glass as a solid barrier, so they fly toward what appears to be open sky or reflected vegetation. Window decals, films, and screens break up the reflective surface and make glass visible to approaching birds. The most effective treatments cover the full window surface at 2-inch spacing, rather than relying on a single large decal. To help prevent window collisions, you can treat the exterior windows in your apartment or home, and advocate for bird-friendly glass treatments for commercial buildings in your community.

## Bird Feeder Tips

Bird feeders can provide an important supplemental food source for birds, particularly during winter when natural food supplies are scarce. They also offer unmatched opportunities for close observation and engagement with local bird communities. To maximize benefit and minimize harm, feeders should be kept clean to prevent disease transmission, placed where they minimize collision risk (either very close to or very far from windows), and stocked with appropriate seeds for the species you hope to attract. This section will cover feeder types, seed selection, placement and maintenance best practices, and guidance on which species to expect at feeders across different regions and seasons in Illinois.

## Conservation Reserve Program

The Conservation Reserve Program (CRP) is a voluntary program run by the U.S. Department of Agriculture Farm Service Agency that works with agricultural producers and landowners to convert plots of agricultural land into land that provides conservation benefits. CRP participants recieve annual rental payments and cost-share assistance to plant approved grasses and other vegetative cover to improve water quality, reduce erosion, and create wildlife habitat.
