## Species Description
Despite the male’s colorful red and black plumage, the Scarlet Tanager is a difficult bird to spot. Because it spends much of its time in the upper canopy, this species is more often heard than seen. Scarlet Tanagers breed in the eastern U.S. north of the Gulf states to southeastern Canada. Scarlet Tanagers are found most frequently in large mature upland and bottomland forests. In small forest blocks this species has high rates of predation and brood parasitism, which lower reproductive success (Mowbray 1999). Scarlet Tanagers are monogamous and aggressively defend territories, the size of which varies depending on the amount of forest area, location, and vegetation type (Robinson 1992, Mowbray 1999). Nests are usually placed at the distal portion of a large branch of a large tree in the mid-story level of the canopy and often in a clump of leaves that provides some screening from above (Prescott 1965). Throughout their range, nests are parasitized by Brown-headed Cowbirds. In woodlots studied in Illinois parasitism rates ranged from 75 to 100% (Brawn and Robinson 1996). In the past three decades the population overall seems to be stable (Mowbray 1999).

## Fast Facts
### Rangewide Distribution:
extreme southeastern Canada, south through the eastern U.S. to northwestern South America
### Illinois Abundance:
fairly common migrant and summer resident
### Conservation Status:
None
### Diet:
insects, invertebrates, fruits
### Breeding Habitat:
deciduous and mixed forests and woodlands

## Phenology
### Nest:
loosely built saucer of grass, rootlets, forbs, and twigs lined with finer materials, in a tree
### Eggs:
4, greenish to bluish, marked with browns, often wreathed
### Incubation Period:
13–14 days
### Time to Fledge:
from 9 to 11 days

## Illinois Population Trends
### History
A century ago, the Scarlet Tanager was a common summer resident of Illinois (Cory 1909). Ridgway (1889) noted “In the southern half of Illinois . . . while not an uncommon summer resident . . . [it] is decidedly a less abundant bird than the Summer [Tanager] Redbird.” This species is most often found in the larger, contiguous forests.
