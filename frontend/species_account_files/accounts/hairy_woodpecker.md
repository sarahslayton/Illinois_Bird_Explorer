## Species Description
This species is a nonmigratory resident throughout most of the U.S. and Canada and the highlands of Mexico and Central America; it is one of the most widely distributed woodpeckers in North America. The Hairy Woodpecker looks and sounds like the smaller Downy Woodpecker but is more likely to be found in larger, mature forests and woodlands. The presence of dead trees and dead branches on live trees is essential for nesting and feeding. The Hairy Woodpecker usually nests in the interior of forests using cavities excavated on the underside of a limb. Since they are shy and fly off when approached, the best way to detect the presence of a Hairy Woodpecker is its sharp, high-pitched, piercing call or rattle. Although fairly common throughout its range, steady declines in the Hairy Woodpecker population have occurred in eastern North America, likely due to forest fragmentation, loss of old growth, and competition for nest sites from European Starlings (Jackson et al. 2002).

## Fast Facts
### Rangewide Distribution:
southeastern Alaska and most of Canada, south throughout most of the U.S. to western Panama
### Illinois Abundance:
fairly common permanent resident
### Conservation Status:
None
### Diet:
wood-boring insects, nuts, fruits, seeds, and tree sap
### Breeding Habitat:
deciduous forests and woodlands with dead limbs and snags

## Phenology
### Nest:
an excavated tree cavity lined with chips
### Eggs:
4, white, unmarked
### Incubation Period:
11–15 days
### Time to Fledge:
from 24 to 30 days

## Illinois Population Trends
### History
Nineteenth and early twentieth century accounts of Hairy Woodpeckers in Illinois refer to them as common permanent residents (Ridgway 1889; Cory 1909). However, Barnes (1912) suggested the population was declining. Graber et al. (1977) believed that the Hairy Woodpecker population was certainly declining during the late 1950s through early 1970s. The Hairy Woodpecker population is currently estimated to be about one-fifth the size of the Downy population (Kleen 2000b).
