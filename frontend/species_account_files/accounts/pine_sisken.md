Pine Siskin	      
Carduelis pinus

Species Description
Pine Siskins breed in the western and northern U.S., western and southern Canada, and parts of Mexico; sporadic breeding occurs as far south as a line from Oklahoma to New Jersey and in the Appalachian Mountains. Known for nomadic and irruptive winter wanderings, Pine Siskins are sometimes common and other times rare or absent at a given location, possibly as a response to variability in the abundance of seeds. These small finches primarily inhabit coniferous or mixed coniferous-deciduous habitats in forests, parks, and residential areas. The gregarious siskins often forage in flocks and are attracted to bird feeders with sunflower or thistle seeds. They nest in loose colonies (Weaver and West 1943; Dawson 1997) and nests are usually built in conifers and concealed in the foliage (Bohlen 1989). Pair formation and nesting may begin as early as late February. In cold climates nesting females are fed by their mates while incubating in order to protect the eggs and young from freezing temperatures (Dawson 1997). 
	
Fast Facts
Rangewide Distribution: southern Alaska and the southern half of Canada, south through most of the U.S. to central Mexico. 
Illinois Abundance: common (irregular) migrant and winter resident, occasional summer resident. 
Conservation Status: Partners in Flight Common Bird in Steep Decline
Diet: seeds, insects.
Breeding Habitat: coniferous and mixed coniferous-deciduous forests, woodlands, parks, suburbs.

Phenology
Nest: a saucer of twigs, rootlets, and grass lined with fine rootlets, moss, fur, and feathers, in a tree.
Eggs: 3–4, pale greenish blue, spotted with browns or black, usually wreathed.
Incubation: 13 days.
Fledging: from 14 to 15 days.

Illinois History 
Pine Siskins regularly breed as far south as Minnesota and Wisconsin but breeding attempts in Illinois are apparently recent. Early accounts of the Pine Siskin in Illinois are non-existent. Chapel (1984) provided a detailed chronology of Pine Siskin nests in Illinois, the first of which occurred in McLean County in 1973. Between 1976 and 1981 four more nesting attempts were made and two of those resulted in successfully fledged young. In 1982 a number of breeding attempts were reported, including eight in Urbana (Champaign County) and four at widely scattered locations in northern Illinois; about half of these nests were successful. Pine Siskin continues to be a rare and sporadic breeding species in Illinois. Most, if not all, Illinois nests have been found in conifers.
